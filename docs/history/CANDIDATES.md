# Round-2 candidates (8, un-vetted)

The adversarial vetting panel died on a session limit before it examined any of these.

## Cutaway  —  *event-attached*

**Pitch.** The moment a video lands on your computer, Cutaway offers it back trimmed, captioned, and ready to send thirty seconds later — and nothing ever leaves your machine.

**Claimed moat.** Every incumbent is economically locked to the cloud: Descript uploads all media and charges $16-50/mo, Clipchamp exists to drive OneDrive/M365 attach, Filmora sells cloud AI credits, and CapCut monetizes user content itself via its ToS — plus its US geoblock is a regulatory wall no ByteDance response can fix. A $40-once, nothing-leaves-your-computer finisher is business-model-hostile to all of them, and Recut (solo dev, Mac+Windows, one-time price) proves the exact business shape.


## Riffle  —  *daily-generated-ritual*

**Pitch.** Solitaire reborn as a daily puzzle: one solver-verified deal with a fresh rule twist, the whole world plays the same hand — buy it once, zero ads forever.

**Claimed moat.** Microsoft cannot un-enshittify: the interstitials across 35M players and the $1.49/mo removal subscription ARE the business, and a clean buy-once release torches that revenue line. NYT and LinkedIn need puzzles as subscription and engagement funnels on their own platforms, not one-time Steam SKUs. And the channel itself punishes subscription dailies (GeoGuessr's Steam launch: 15% positive, near-worst in platform history) while rewarding exactly this shape. Too small for a clean BigCo play, forbidden for the incumbent.


## Familiar  —  *ambient*

**Pitch.** A little creature lives at the edge of your screen and visibly thrives on your good days — it somehow just knows how your day is going, without you logging anything.

**Claimed moat.** Opal and Jomo are welded to Apple's Screen Time APIs, which do not exist on Windows — following means a ground-up rebuild on a platform they've ignored for years. Microsoft frames all screen-time as parental control and cannot tell adults they doomscrolled while its own Edge/MSN feed sells the doomscroll. The 10-15 funded focus startups need SaaS economics, not a $10 Steam toy with cosmetic DLC — Rize fled to B2B, Focus Bear retreated to an ADHD niche. OS vendors absorb features but never character economies (the f.lux law), and the free-blocker floor (ScreenZen, SelfControl) commoditizes blocking, not companionship — this blocks nothing, so it is outside that race to zero.


## Courier  —  *ride-a-moment*

**Pitch.** A fast, quiet email app for Windows and Mac that opens instantly and shows your mail and nothing else — no ads dressed up as emails, no assistant — and costs $30 once.

**Claimed moat.** Microsoft cannot ship this — new Outlook exists to serve ads and upsell M365, so a calm ad-free client cannibalizes its own model; Google refuses native clients to protect webmail ad views; and the VC cohort just exited the inbox for agents (Notion Mail dead September 22, 2026 — a dated, addressable switching moment landing exactly at launch time; Superhuman swallowed by Grammarly) because $30-once is too small for them. Too small for BigCo, wrong shape for VCs, perfect for one bootstrapper.


## Sixes  —  *daily-generated-ritual*

**Pitch.** Six fresh trivia questions a day, played head-to-head in a small league with your friends — pub trivia that fits in a coffee break.

**Claimed moat.** The moat is the league graph, not the questions. NYT cannot publish machine-generated factual content under its masthead while litigating against AI companies — its games are hand-edited by identity; LinkedIn needs career-safe framing and trivia has none; both deliberately ship solitary streak games because leagues require identity, matchmaking, and moderation that don't fit a subscription-funnel or feed-engagement model. LearnedLeague's owner has kept it invite-only and one-man for 29 years. Once your five friends are in your league, a better question generator elsewhere is worthless — generation is commodity, so the social shape is the only defensible layer.


## Morrow  —  *ambient*

**Pitch.** A tiny world in the corner of your screen that lives one real day at a time — every morning something happened overnight, and one small decision is waiting for you.

**Claimed moat.** Wallpaper Engine's decade moat is a Workshop of static user-made assets — per-user living worlds break its UGC economics, and it has never shipped macOS in ten years. Desktop Mate competes on licensed IP (a treadmill it cannot leave, and $14.99 Miku packs a solo dev needn't fight). Apple and Microsoft absorb features, never toys with economies (the f.lux sherlock law), and the 249-title desktop-pet flood is pure decoration with no generation pipeline behind it.


## Porchlight  —  *event-attached*

**Pitch.** Porchlight quietly watches Mom's inbox and browser and speaks up in plain English the moment something is a scam — before she clicks, and her adult kids can see the porch light is on.

**Claimed moat.** Norton and McAfee cannot ship a simple cheap standalone without cannibalizing their $30-100/yr fear-driven suite bundles (Genie got folded INTO Norton 360, proving it); Google won't add cross-channel family alerting to Gmail — reading grandma's mail for her daughter is privacy-radioactive for an ad company; Microsoft frames all protection as enterprise or parental control; Edge's scareware blocker lives in the blocking layer, not the judgment-and-coaching layer; and Carefull chose the bank B2B2C channel, leaving the direct family desktop app empty.


## Inklings  —  *daily-generated-ritual*

**Pitch.** A ten-minute daily writing dare — everyone gets the same tiny challenge, you write your take, then read three strangers' takes and crown one.

**Claimed moat.** Every writing incumbent monetizes AI writing FOR you — Grammarly, Notion, Sudowrite, and AI-first Duolingo (which took a mass streak-quitting revolt for it) — so a strictly human-written arena is a position they cannot occupy without contradicting their own product. NaNoWriMo died partly for defending AI; this cohort's loyalty goes to whoever credibly bans machine prose. A human-only brand plus community trust is unfollowable by precisely the companies rich enough to follow.
