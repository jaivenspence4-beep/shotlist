# Draft: which of the 8 to build

Read `CANDIDATES.md` for the full pitches and claimed moats.

## The bar these have to clear

Jaiven's criteria, from a long back-and-forth where four earlier proposals were
rejected:

1. A standalone destination app ordinary people adopt without thinking twice
2. People keep coming back — not something they forget exists
3. Nobody has it yet
4. Cross-platform Mac **and** Windows — explicitly not Apple-specific
5. Uses new capability as invisible plumbing, never as the pitch
6. Not too cutting edge — no exotic hardware, no unproven standards
7. Not something already worked on together (CareLog, Oddling, LyricGlass, Sentinel,
   GLaDOS)

Round 1 killed all six of its candidates and produced three laws worth applying here:
loudly-begged-for universal pains already have 10–15 funded startups; desktop apps die
when the trigger moment happens away from the computer; manual-feeding loops kill —
data must be pushed or already on-machine.

## My read

**Recommend: Riffle** (daily solitaire, one solver-verified deal with a rule twist,
buy-once, no ads). It is the only candidate that clears criteria 1 and 2 without an
argument: solitaire needs no explanation to any adult on earth, and the daily-shared-
deal is the Wordle mechanic, which is the strongest retention shape on the list. Steam
and the MS Store are real distribution. Its weakness is criterion 3 — "nobody has it"
is nearly false, since solitaire is the most cloned genre in software.

**Second: Cutaway** (local trim + caption on any video that lands). Strongest genuine
technology story, cleanest riding-a-moment trigger, and Recut proves the exact business
shape works for a solo dev. Its weakness is criterion 1 — video editing is not
universal, and CapCut and Clipchamp are free and everywhere.

**Third: Porchlight** (scam warning for a parent's machine). Real unmet need with a
clear payer. Its weakness is criterion 2 in a way I think is fatal: the buyer installs
it on someone else's computer and then never opens it again.

**The five I would drop.** Courier — email clients are a graveyard (Sparrow, Newton,
Astro, Polymail) and Thunderbird is free and improving. Sixes and Inklings — both need
friends already present, so cold start is the whole game. Familiar and Morrow — ambient
desktop pets are charming demos with unproven retention, and screen-time guilt apps
retain badly.

## What I want attacked

- Is Riffle's failure on criterion 3 disqualifying, or is "no ads, buy once, one shared
  daily deal" a real enough wedge given Microsoft cannot follow without torching its own
  ad revenue?
- Is my Cutaway adoption worry right, or am I underrating how often ordinary people now
  have a video they need to trim and caption?
- Am I wrong to drop Courier on graveyard evidence alone? Graveyards sometimes mean the
  timing was early, not that the market is closed.
- Is there a candidate here whose claimed moat is simply false on the facts? The moats
  were written by the agents that proposed them and have not been checked by anyone.
